All other letters except the ones listed below have same pronunciation as English.
1. `a` on its own is pronounced /ə/ (`अ`) while after a consonant it has the impact of /æ/ (`आ`).
2. `ä` (a-umlaut) is pronounced (`ऐ`).
3. `ch` is a bit loaded.
    1. `tsch` is pronounced /tʃ/ (`च`).
    2. `sch` is pronounced /ʃ/ (`श`).
    3. `ch` if preceded by `i` or `e` or `ö` sound is pronounced /ç/. This sound is non-existent in Hindi. One can approximate it to /h/ (`ह`). A more accurate pronunciation would be to say `ह` while touching back of one's tongue to the upper palette (ceiling of the mouth).
    4. In all other cases, it is pronounced /x/ (`ख़`).
4. `d` at the end of a word is pronounced /t/ `त` while everywhere else it is like the English IPA /d/ `द`. So, `kind` (child) is `किंत` while `kinder`(children) is `किंदर`.
5. `eu` is pronounced `ओ ॅ ए` (diphthong). This sound is not native to Hindi. It is half-way between `ओए` and `औए`.
6. `ei` is pronounced (`आइ`) (diphthong).
7. `e`, if it is the first vowel sound, is pronounced `ई`. If it is somewhere in the middle, it is `ॅ`. And if it is at the end, it is `अ` (schwa).
8. `ie` is pronounced /i/ (`ई`).
9. `h` is like English `h` /h/ (`ह`) except after vowels, it acts as a sound extender like Sanskrit avagraha `ऽ`.
10. `j` is pronounced like English `y` /j/ (`य`)
11. `g` at the end of a word is pronounced as `क`. Everywhere else, it is same as English `g` /g/ (`ग`).
12. `ng` is pronounced like English `ng` /ŋ/ (`ङ`).
13. `r`, just like English, if preceded by a vowel extends the previous sound (`ऽ`). Otherwise, it sounds like English retroflex `r` (Sanskrit `ऋ`).
14. `s` when followed by a vowel is pronounced `ज़`. `s` when followed by consonants is pronounced `श`. `s` at the end is pronounced `स`.
15. `t` is pronounced `त` - like Spanish.
16. `o` is pronounced `ऑ`. Think of the first sound in `मॉडल`. This sound is not native to Hindi either and it is half-way between `मोडल` and `मौडल`.
17. `ö` is pronounced like `i` in `bird`. This sound is non-existent in pure Hindi. And the `ॅ` is used to donate it these days.
18. `u` is pronounced /u/ (`ऊ`).
19. `ü` is pronounced /Y/ `ऊ` with the sound pronounced more in the throat
20. `v` is pronounced like English f /f/ (`फ़`). And that's why Volkswagen is pronounced फ़ोल्क्सवागन. While such consonant clusters might be rare in English, they are common in German. German has `f` as well, and that's pronounced like English `f` too! Now, for English loan words, v is pronounced as /v/ :/
21. `w` is pronounced like English /v/, (`व़`)
22. `z` is pronounced /ts/ (`त्स`)
23. `ß` is pronounced as long s sound - `सऽ`. This is the only non-Latin alphabet in German.
